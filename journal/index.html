2. The numbers

Six things. Read them off your chart or your broker.

Did you buy or sell?

Buying first is a long — you want the price to rise. Selling first is a short.

Where did you get in?

The price you were actually filled at, not the one you hoped for.

Where was your stop?

The price where you would have admitted the idea was wrong and got out.

How many shares?

The size of the position. This is what turns a price gap into an amount of money.

Where were you aiming to get out?

Your target. Knowing it beforehand is how you tell whether the trade was worth the risk at all.

Where did you get out?

Leave this if you are still holding — everything you have already decided can still be graded.

3. Three honest questions

Nobody sees these. They predict an emptied account better than any chart does, so they are worth answering truthfully even when the answer is annoying.

Did you decide the stop before you got in?

A stop worked out afterwards is a reaction. Deciding beforehand, while you are calm, is the whole point of having one.

Did you move your stop further away?

Moving it toward profit is fine. Moving it away turns a planned small loss into an unplanned large one, and it is the habit that ends accounts.

Did you write down why, before entering?

Written afterwards it is a story that fits the result. Written beforehand it is something you can actually review.
