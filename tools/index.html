Calculators

The numbers, worked out for you

Six calculators. The first one is the only one you need every single time: before you place a trade, work out your position size.

These are the same sums the lessons walk through, so you can check your own arithmetic in a few seconds. Change any box and the answer updates as you type. Where a lesson has a worked example, you can load it and compare.

A calculator can tell you how much to buy and what it would cost you to be wrong. It cannot tell you whether the trade is worth taking. That judgement is yours, and it is built from your own record of what you did and what happened afterwards.

Position size

How many shares to take so that hitting your stop costs exactly what you intended.

(account × risk %) ÷ (entry − stop)

Shares
250
Risking 1% of $25,000 with a $1 stop means 250 shares. The answer should come out at 250

Where this comes from: Position sizing

Same sum, drawn instead of typed. The boxes above and the chart below work out the position exactly the same way — the chart just lets you put the stop somewhere and see what it costs you.

Risk check

What percentage of your account a position you are already planning would actually risk.

(size × (entry − stop)) ÷ account × 100

Answer
1.00%
250 shares with a $1 stop risks $250, which is 1% of $25,000. The answer should come out at 1.00%

Where this comes from: Risk comes first, always

Reward:risk

How many times your risk you stand to make if the target is reached.

(target − entry) ÷ (entry − stop)

Answer
3.00
A $3 target against a $1 stop is 3:1. The answer should come out at 3.00

Where this comes from: Measuring in R

Breakeven win rate

How often you need to be right, at a given reward:risk, just to break even.

1 ÷ (1 + reward:risk) × 100

Answer
33.33%
At 3:1 you can be wrong three times out of four and still break even. The answer should come out at 25.00%

Where this comes from: Measuring in R

Same two sums, drawn instead of typed. Moving the target on this chart changes the reward:risk in the calculator above it, and the win rate you need follows from that one number.

Expectancy

What an average trade is worth, in R, given a win rate and a reward:risk.

(win % × reward) − (loss % × 1)

Answer
0.20
Winning 40% of the time at 2:1 is worth 0.2R per trade — a real edge. The answer should come out at 0.20

Where this comes from: Measuring in R

Losing streak cost

What a run of consecutive losses does to your account at a given risk per trade.

(1 − (1 − risk %)^losses) × 100

Answer
9.56%
Ten losses at 10% per trade costs 65% of the account. At 1% it costs 9.6%. The answer should come out at 65.13%

Where this comes from: Risk comes first, always
